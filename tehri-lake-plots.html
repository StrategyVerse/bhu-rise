Tehri Lake plots: the Delhi weekend-home play — and the land law you must clear first.

Residential Land8 min readFor Delhi-NCR investors

The short answer: Tehri is one of the more compelling hill second-home options for a Delhi-NCR buyer — a large lake, cooler air, and improving road access as the Delhi–Dehradun Expressway matures. But Uttarakhand tightened its land law for non-residents in 2025, and buying here now means working within those limits. Get the legal position right first; the view is only worth it on a title you can actually hold.

Why Tehri appeals to an NCR buyer

The second-home pull from Delhi-NCR is strong and well-documented — NCR is India's largest source of second-home demand, and the "48-hour city" buyer (weekdays in the city, weekends in the hills) is now a real segment. Tehri fits that brief:

  • Tehri Lake — one of the largest man-made lakes in the country — gives the location a genuine, scarce amenity, not just a view.
  • Connectivity is improving. Infrastructure upgrades, including the Delhi–Dehradun Expressway and Char Dham road works, are cutting hill travel times from NCR.
  • Lifestyle + asset. A hill plot can serve as a weekend retreat, a remote-work escape, and a long-term land holding at once.

The part most sellers won't lead with: Uttarakhand's 2025 land law

In February 2025, Uttarakhand's cabinet approved a stricter land law (Bhu-Kanoon) aimed at protecting state resources. For a non-resident buyer, the headline points reported are:

  • Outsiders are barred from buying agricultural and horticultural land in 11 of the state's 13 districts (the two plains districts, Haridwar and Udham Singh Nagar, are the exceptions).
  • Residential purchase limits apply — reporting cites roughly 250 sq m for residential (and around 500 sq m for commercial) for non-residents, often framed as a once-in-a-lifetime allowance outside municipal areas.
  • Approval now sits with the state government, not the district magistrate, for purchases needing permission.
  • An affidavit declaring intended land use is reported to be mandatory, with a digital portal tracking outsider purchases.

Verify before you rely: sources conflict on the exact square-metre limits and on whether Tehri specifically is treated like the other restricted hill districts. Before you commit, check the gazetted amendment text and the revenue department notification for Tehri Garhwal directly — do not rely on a broker's summary (or ours).

What this means in practice

  • A big "farmland" or large hill parcel as an outsider is likely off the table in restricted districts without state permission — and permission is now harder to get.
  • A modest residential plot within the allowed limit may still be buyable — which actually suits a weekend-home buyer more than a land-banker.
  • Structure matters. How the purchase is framed (residential use, within limits, correct declarations) is now central to whether it is even legal.

The honest case against

  • Legal friction is real. The 2025 law deliberately makes large outsider purchases harder; this is not the open field it was a few years ago.
  • Hill-specific diligence. Slope stability, approach-road legality, water, and construction permissions in eco-sensitive zones all need checking — a flat NCR plot this is not.
  • Liquidity is thin. Resale in hill micro-markets can be slow; buy for use and patience, not a quick exit.
  • Connectivity is a promise in progress. Expressway benefits are real but still maturing; underwrite today's drive time, not the brochure's.

How to approach a Tehri plot

  1. Confirm the legal position first — district status, residential limit, and permission needs — before falling for a specific view.
  2. Buy within the residential allowance for a genuine weekend-home use, rather than attempting a large holding that triggers (or fails) the permission regime.
  3. Run hill-grade diligence — title, land-use class, approach road, slope, water, and buildability.
  4. Underwrite it as a lifestyle asset first, appreciation second. The lake and the air are the return you can count on.

Our posture: in Uttarakhand today we start with the law, not the listing. If a parcel can't be held cleanly and legally by a non-resident for its intended use, no view justifies it.

Frequently asked questions

Can an outsider buy land in Tehri / Uttarakhand in 2026?

Non-residents can generally still buy a limited-size residential plot, but Uttarakhand's 2025 land law bars outsiders from buying agricultural/horticultural land in 11 of 13 districts and routes larger purchases through state-government permission. Verify Tehri's specific status against the gazetted amendment before buying.

How much land can a non-resident buy in Uttarakhand?

Reporting cites roughly 250 sq m for residential and about 500 sq m for commercial use for non-residents, often as a once-in-a-lifetime allowance outside municipal limits. The exact figures vary by source — confirm against the official notification.

Is Tehri a good place for a weekend home from Delhi?

Yes, for buyers who value the lake, cooler climate and improving connectivity via the Delhi–Dehradun Expressway, and who buy a legally compliant residential plot. It suits lifestyle-first ownership more than large-scale land banking under the current law.

What should I check before buying a hill plot in Tehri?

The 2025 land-law position for non-residents, clear title and land-use classification, a legal approach road, slope/soil stability, water availability, and construction permissions in eco-sensitive zones.
